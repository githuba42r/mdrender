Privacy policy

Last updated 3 October 2026. This policy covers both parts of MDRender: the Android app and any cloud push server it pairs with (together, the “Service”). Each server is run by its own operator (the “operator”).

1. The two parts of the Service

The MDRender Android app is a document viewer that runs entirely on your device: your files stay there, encrypted, and the app contains no analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting code. An optional Cloud Push feature pairs the app with a cloud push server so the web console and your other devices can deliver notifications and files to it. Pairing is off until you choose it, and can be undone at any time.

2. What the Android app does

  • Files you import are stored encrypted on the device (AES-256 with keys in the Android Keystore). They are never uploaded, and the app transmits no usage data, analytics, or telemetry.
  • When you pair via Cloud Push, the app sends the server: the pairing code scanned from the QR, the device name and model, a Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) registration token so notifications can wake the app, and device public keys for encryption.
  • Notification messages are delivered through Google FCM; the message itself carries no file content, only the cue to fetch. Pushed files are downloaded from the server and decrypted on the device when end-to-end encryption is on — the server never sees the plaintext.
  • If your server is a self-hosted federated server, it registers with the cloud push master, and the master sees nothing from it but an encrypted wake-up ping with opaque device identifiers. Files and their metadata stay on your server; no data leaves it to the master.
  • The optional LocalSend feature transfers files to devices on your local network only; nothing leaves your network.

3. What the cloud push server collects

  • Account details — your email address and a salted hash of your password.
  • Devices — the names and models you give them when pairing, their FCM tokens, pairing and encryption keys, and activity timestamps.
  • Content you send — messages and files pushed to your devices, including anything waiting to be delivered. Pushes to an encrypted device are end-to-end encrypted: the operator sees an unreadable, opaque blob — not the file contents, names, folders, or types.
  • Billing records — orders, top-ups, credit movements, and PayPal transaction identifiers, amounts and dates.
  • Technical logs — IP addresses and request logs, used for security, ban enforcement and diagnosing faults.
  • A session cookie — to keep you signed in.

4. How it is used

  • to run the Service: delivering pushes, storing what is pending, and showing your devices, pushes and history;
  • to manage your account and answer support requests;
  • to bill correctly: credit balances, metered usage and payment records;
  • to keep the Service safe: detecting abuse, enforcing bans, and investigating faults.

5. Payments

Payments are handled by PayPal under PayPal’s own privacy policy. The server receives only the payment outcome, amount, date and a transaction reference — card numbers never reach the server.

6. Sharing

Personal data is not sold. It is shared only with Google (FCM delivery of wake-up messages to paired devices, under Google’s policies), with PayPal to process payments, with a federated peer server if the operator has federation enabled (limited server metadata: identifier, hostname, public key and last-seen time), and where the operator is legally required to disclose it.

7. Retention

The app keeps your files and settings only on your device: deleting them in the app or uninstalling removes them. On the server, data is kept while your account is active. Undelivered pushes are purged after they are delivered or expire. Billing records are kept as long as needed for accounting. When an account is deleted, its data is removed within a reasonable period, except records the operator must keep by law.

8. Security

On the device: files are encrypted at rest, backups of app data are disabled, an app lock (biometric or PIN) is available, and the window can be protected from screenshots. On the server: connections are encrypted with TLS, passwords are stored as hashes, and where a device is encrypted, pushes and files are end-to-end encrypted so the operator cannot read them. No system is perfectly secure, and the operator cannot guarantee absolute security.

9. Your choices

You can turn Cloud Push off and unpair the device from the app at any time, manage your details from the account pages, revoke any paired device from the server, and ask the operator to delete your account (confirmed by email, held for a grace period, then purged). You may also request a copy of the personal data held about you.

10. Cookies

The Service uses a single session cookie to keep you signed in on the web console. There are no third-party advertising or tracking cookies on the console.

11. Changes and contact

This policy may be updated from time to time; the date at the top shows when it last changed. Questions about the server, or requests about your data, go to the operator of the server you paired with (each deployment publishes its own support contact). Questions about the Android app may be raised at github.com/githuba42r/mdrender.
